WHO Recommended Dietary Limits for Sodium

verified High Retrieval Confidence (98.4%)
info

The World Health Organization recommends that adults consume less than 2,000 mg of sodium per day (equivalent to just under 5 grams or 1 level teaspoon of table salt).

In contrast, the average American currently consumes approximately 3,400 mg to 3,600 mg of sodium daily—over 70% above the recommended target.

Are potassium-based salt substitutes safe for individuals with kidney issues?

⚠️ Clinical Prescription Advisory

Potassium chloride salt substitutes can cause dangerous, life-threatening hyperkalemia in individuals with Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) or those taking ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or potassium-sparing diuretics.

While potassium-enriched salts (typically substituting 25%–30% of sodium chloride with potassium chloride) have proven remarkably effective in reducing stroke incidents in standard hypertensive populations, impaired renal function hinders clearance of excess circulating potassium.

Recommended Protocol

  • Consult your nephrologist: Always seek direct lab verification of eGFR and serum electrolyte levels before testing any low-sodium salts.
  • Prioritize culinary acid & aromatics: Utilize lemon juice, apple cider vinegar, fresh dill, or sumac to mimic salty pungency without raising potassium load.
  • Check packaged seasonings: Scrutinize spice blends for hidden potassium chloride additives (often marketed as "light salt" or "mineral salt").
